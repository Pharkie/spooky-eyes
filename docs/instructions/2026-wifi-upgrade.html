1Switch Spooky Eyes off.
2Move the wires. Move each wire from the old Pico to the new ESP32-C3, by where the wire’s other end goes. Your wire colours may not match the drawing (except red and black, which are standard).

The mouth LED’s long leg (+) must go on 3.3. Short legs mixed up? Any order works: the mouth just shows other colours. Match the pin names printed on the ESP32-C3.

3Bring yourself back online, Dolores. Switch Spooky Eyes on. The eyes start their show. It’s alive!

4Introduce Spooky Eyes to your WiFi (optional). On a phone, tablet or laptop, join the Spooky Pumpkin WiFi (above). After a few seconds, a setup page should open: name your pumpkin, pick your home WiFi (2.4 GHz), enter the WiFi password and tap Check and save. Your WiFi password never leaves Spooky Eyes.
5Play. Spooky Eyes restarts, joins your WiFi, scrolls the address once and carries on with the show. Open http://pumpkin.local on a phone, tablet or laptop on your WiFi (on Android, type the scrolled numbers instead).

Setup page won’t open? Forget Spooky Pumpkin in your phone’s WiFi settings, switch Spooky Eyes off and on, then join again (switching mobile data off can help).

Page won’t open later? WiFi sleeps after 10 unused minutes: switch Spooky Eyes off and on.

On the doorstep? Out of reach of your WiFi for 3 minutes, Spooky Eyes brings back the Spooky Pumpkin WiFi: join and open 192.168.4.1. Back in range, Spooky Eyes rejoins your WiFi by itself.

BOOT button (on the board): press to repeat the address; hold 5 seconds to forget your home WiFi.
